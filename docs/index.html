OpenFlamma/Getting Started/Introduction
Developer preview

The open financial ledger for AI agents in global trade.

A shared financial layer for agents working across trade orders, documents, bank feeds and multi-currency payments—with human approval built in.

7 min readUpdated November 2026
Primary marketBuilt first for importers, exporters and trading companies.

OpenFlamma starts where finance is hardest: one trade order, many documents, several currencies and payments, and no shared operational record.

Preview documentation

The product model and principles are ready to explore. Install commands, endpoint schemas and packages will be published with the first public source release.

v0.1

OpenFlamma is built around the operating reality of trade customers. AI agents are ready to match transactions, draft journal entries, check invoices and follow trade documents—but today’s finance systems give them no shared ledger, no clear authority and no durable record of what they did.

OpenFlamma gives them all three:

01

An open ledger

A double-entry ledger for fiat and digital assets. One source of truth agents can work from and your team can approve.

02

Permissions and policies

Simple rules define what each agent can read, what it can propose, and what needs human approval.

03

A complete audit trail

Every agent action is recorded: what it did, what data it used, who approved it, and when.

Important boundary

OpenFlamma records and organises financial data. It does not hold, move or transfer money.

Mental model

Core Concepts

These concepts describe how people, agents and financial evidence fit together. Names and relationships are stable; implementation details will be added as the source becomes public.

01

Ledger

The single record of every financial event. OpenFlamma uses double-entry bookkeeping: every transaction moves value from one account to another, so the books always balance. Entries are never edited or deleted, only corrected by new entries, so the history remains complete.

02

Accounts

A place where value is recorded, such as a bank account, a customer balance, a supplier balance, revenue or fees. Each account can hold several currencies and digital assets.

03

Transactions

A balanced set of entries across two or more accounts. A transaction can carry references to its source, such as an invoice, a bank statement line or a shipping document.

04

Agents

Any AI agent or automated program that works with the ledger. Each agent has its own identity, so every action can be traced back to it.

05

Policies

Rules that decide what an agent may read, propose or commit—and when a person must approve the action.

06

Human approval

When a policy requires a person, the agent’s work waits in a review queue. A person approves, edits or rejects it. Agents do the work; people stay in control.

07

Audit trail

A permanent log of every action by people and agents: who did it, what changed, what data was used, which policy applied and who approved it.

08

Sources

Where data comes from: bank feeds, payment records, invoices, contracts and trade documents. Every ledger entry can link back to its source.

Design constraints

Our Principles

  1. 01

    Open by default.

    Finance needs proof, not promises. The code that touches your books should be open to inspect.

  2. 02

    Agents do the work, people decide.

    Agents take on repetitive work. Important decisions stay with people.

  3. 03

    Every action is traceable.

    Nothing happens without a record of who did it and why.

  4. 04

    Your data stays yours.

    Self-host it, export it, take it anywhere. No lock-in.

  5. 05

    Built for global trade.

    Multiple currencies, digital assets and cross-border documents belong in the core, not an add-on.

Agents

Permissions, policies and approval

Every agent has an identity and a policy boundary. A policy can make an agent read-only, allow it to draft a specific action, restrict it by account or amount, or require a person before anything is committed.

A policy answers four questions

  • What can this agent read?
  • What can it propose?
  • What can it commit directly?
  • Which actions need a named human role?
conceptual policy · syntax not final
agent: reconciliation-assistant
read:
  - bank_feeds
  - invoices
propose:
  - journal_entries
approval:
  above: USD 10000
  role: finance_manager

This example explains the intended policy model. The final policy schema will be published with the API specification.
